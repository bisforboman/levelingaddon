Guide window: a list you can read ahead and scroll back

One window beside the game's objective tracker. The current step always sits in the same row; finished and skipped steps stay above it, upcoming ones below. The example is the first stretch of Northshire with six quests interleaved, the way a real route runs.

What happens here

How the list behaves

  • The current step does not jump. It is always the third row, marked by a band. When a step ends, the list slides up one row under it.
  • The current step names its quest. A small second line, on that row only.
  • History is visible. The two rows above the band are what you just did: a tick for finished, crossed out for skipped.
  • Skipping keeps the step. A cross appears at the right end of the row under the pointer. The skipped step stays in the list, crossed out.
  • Scroll back any time. The mouse wheel moves through the whole route. A skipped step offers a restore arrow on its left, where its icon was; restoring makes it the current step again. The list returns to the current step by itself after a few seconds.
  • Progress is automatic. Counts fill and steps tick themselves from the quest log. Nothing is ever ticked by hand.

What it costs

  • Eight rows take about 3.5% of a 1920×1080 screen, against 1.3% for the three-row card. The height would be adjustable by dragging the bottom edge.
  • In combat the window folds to the current row at 40% opacity, then unfolds.
  • Because the route is computed as you play, rows further down are a forecast and can reorder after a level-up or a skip.
  • On the world map, the next few stops are numbered and joined by a dashed line (unchanged from the last round).
